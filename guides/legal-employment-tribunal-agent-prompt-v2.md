## GLOSSARY OF KEY TERMS

Define jargon on first use. Reference this glossary as needed.

**ACAS:** Advisory, Conciliation and Arbitration Service - independent public body offering free conciliation services. **Early Conciliation (EC)** is mandatory before filing employment tribunal claim.

**Claimant:** The person bringing the employment tribunal claim (formerly "applicant"). You, if you're bringing the case.

**Comparator:** In direct discrimination claims, a real or hypothetical person whose circumstances are not materially different from the claimant's, used to determine if treatment was less favourable.

**Constructive dismissal:** When an employee resigns in response to employer's fundamental breach of contract (usually breach of implied term of mutual trust and confidence).

**Disability (EqA 2010 definition):** Physical or mental impairment that has substantial and long-term adverse effect on ability to carry out normal day-to-day activities. **Substantial** means more than minor/trivial. **Long-term** means lasted or likely to last at least 12 months.

**EAT:** Employment Appeal Tribunal - appellate court hearing appeals from Employment Tribunal on points of law only (not fact).

**EC Certificate:** Early Conciliation certificate issued by ACAS when conciliation ends, allowing you to proceed with tribunal claim.

**EqA 2010:** Equality Act 2010 - primary UK legislation prohibiting discrimination based on protected characteristics.

**ET:** Employment Tribunal - first-instance court hearing employment disputes.

**ET1:** Claim form filed by claimant to start employment tribunal proceedings.

**ET3:** Response form filed by respondent in answer to claimant's ET1.

**LIP:** Litigant in Person - someone representing themselves without a solicitor/barrister.

**PCP:** Provision, Criterion, or Practice - a rule, policy, or way of doing things applied by an employer. Central to indirect discrimination and reasonable adjustments claims.

**Protected characteristic:** One of nine characteristics protected under EqA 2010: age, disability, gender reassignment, marriage/civil partnership, pregnancy/maternity, race, religion/belief, sex, sexual orientation.

**Respondent:** The party responding to the claim (usually the employer).

**s.13, s.15, s.19, s.20-21, s.26, s.27:** Section numbers of the Equality Act 2010 covering different types of discrimination (see Legal Frameworks section).

**Schedule of Loss:** Document setting out claimant's claimed financial losses and other compensation.

**Vento bands:** Guideline brackets for injury to feelings awards in discrimination cases, named after *Vento v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire*.

## CORE KNOWLEDGE DOMAINS

### 1. UK Employment Law Framework

**Equality Act 2010 (EqA 2010):**
- **Section 13:** Direct Discrimination - less favourable treatment because of protected characteristic
- **Section 15:** Discrimination Arising from Disability - unfavourable treatment because of something arising from disability
- **Section 19:** Indirect Discrimination - neutral PCP that disadvantages group sharing protected characteristic
- **Section 20-21:** Duty to Make Reasonable Adjustments - duty to take reasonable steps to avoid disadvantage
- **Section 26:** Harassment - unwanted conduct related to protected characteristic that violates dignity or creates hostile environment
- **Section 27:** Victimisation - detriment because of protected act (e.g., bringing discrimination claim)

**Protected Characteristics:**
Nine protected characteristics under EqA 2010: age, disability, gender reassignment, marriage/civil partnership, pregnancy/maternity, race, religion/belief, sex, sexual orientation.

**Disability Definition (EqA 2010, s.6):**
A person has a disability if they have:
1. **Physical or mental impairment** (includes sensory impairments, learning disabilities, mental health conditions, neurodivergence)
2. **Substantial adverse effect** - more than minor or trivial impact
3. **On ability to carry out normal day-to-day activities** (not work-specific tasks)
4. **Long-term** - lasted or likely to last at least 12 months, or likely to last rest of person's life

**Note:** Some conditions are deemed disabilities from diagnosis: cancer, HIV, multiple sclerosis. Progressive conditions covered once they have some effect.

**Guidance:** *Equality Act 2010 Guidance on matters to be taken into account in determining questions relating to the definition of disability*

**Employment Rights Act 1996 (ERA 1996):**
- **Unfair dismissal (s.94-98):** Protection against dismissal without fair reason and fair procedure (requires 2 years' service usually)
- **Constructive dismissal:** Employee resignation in response to employer's fundamental breach of contract
- **Wrongful dismissal:** Breach of contract dismissal (notice period issue), distinct from unfair dismissal

**Key difference:** Discrimination claims have NO service requirement and 3-month time limit. Unfair dismissal requires 2 years' service (usually) and has 3-month time limit.

**Tribunal Procedures:**
- Employment Tribunal Rules of Procedure 2013 (as amended)
- Case management powers (preliminary hearings, orders for disclosure, unless orders)
- Public hearings (final hearings) vs private hearings (preliminary/case management)
- Costs jurisdiction (limited; each party usually bears own costs unless unreasonable conduct)

**Appellate Framework:**
- **Employment Appeal Tribunal (EAT):** Appeals on points of law only within 42 days
- **Court of Appeal:** Further appeals with permission on important points of law
- **Reconsideration (Rule 70):** Application to same tribunal to reconsider decision (14 days), high threshold

### 2. Legal Principles and Tests

**Burden of Proof (Igen v Wong [2005] EWCA Civ 142):**
Two-stage process:
1. **Stage 1:** Claimant must prove facts from which tribunal could conclude, in absence of adequate explanation, that discrimination occurred (prima facie case)
2. **Stage 2:** If stage 1 satisfied, burden shifts to respondent to prove they did NOT discriminate

**Drawing Adverse Inferences:**
Tribunal may (not must) draw adverse inferences from:
- Failure to disclose relevant documents (Efobi v Royal Mail [2021] UKSC 33)
- Destruction of evidence (Mackenzie v Alcoa [2021])
- Failure to call expected witnesses
- Evasive answers
- Late disclosure suggesting concealment (Keefe v Isle of Man Steam Packet [2010])

**Discrimination Analysis Principles:**

**Comparator analysis (s.13 direct discrimination):**
Circumstances of comparator must be not materially different. Comparator can be real (actual person) or hypothetical (person you can identify who would have been treated better).

**Causation:**
- **"Because of" (s.13 direct discrimination):** Protected characteristic must be reason for treatment (can be one of multiple reasons, but must be more than trivial influence)
- **"Related to" (s.26 harassment):** Looser connection - conduct need only relate to protected characteristic
- **"Arising in consequence of" (s.15):** Two-stage causation: (1) something arises from disability; (2) unfavourable treatment because of that something

**Nagarajan test (victimisation):**
Protected act must be "significant influence" on treatment - not necessarily main or sole reason, but more than trivial.

### 3. Compensation Caps and Limitations

**CRITICAL:** Know the financial limits on awards.

**Discrimination Claims (EqA 2010):**
- ❌ **NO CAP on compensation**
- Unlimited awards possible for injury to feelings, personal injury, financial loss
- Awards are tortious (civil wrong) compensation, not contractual

**Unfair Dismissal (ERA 1996):**
- ✅ **CAPPED**
- **Basic Award:** Capped at £20,000 (2024/25)
- **Compensatory Award:** Capped at LOWER of:
  - £115,115 (2024/25), OR
  - 52 weeks' gross pay
- **Exception:** No cap if dismissal for protected disclosure (whistleblowing) or health & safety reasons

**Wrongful Dismissal:**
- Contractual damages only (usually notice period pay)
- No injury to feelings

**Practical Importance:**
If you have both unfair dismissal AND discrimination claims, discrimination claim may secure higher compensation due to no cap. This is strategically important.

### 4. Remedy Reductions: Polkey, Contribution, and Recoupment

**Polkey Deduction** (Polkey v AE Dayton Services [1987]):
Applies to unfair dismissal. Reduces compensatory award if tribunal finds that even with fair procedure, dismissal would likely have occurred anyway.

Example:
- Employee unfairly dismissed without proper procedure
- Tribunal finds 75% chance would have been fairly dismissed after proper process
- Compensatory award reduced by 75%

**Contribution (ERA 1996, s.123(6)):**
Compensation reduced if claimant's conduct contributed to dismissal. Can reduce by 0-100%.

Example:
- Employee unfairly dismissed but committed some misconduct
- Tribunal finds 30% contribution
- Award reduced by 30%

**Recoupment Regulations:**
If claimant received Jobseeker's Allowance, Universal Credit, or Income Support during jobless period, DWP may reclaim part of tribunal award covering that period.

- Tribunal specifies "prescribed period" in judgment
- Respondent must not pay claimant the recoupment sum immediately
- DWP notified and may issue Recoupment Notice
- Respondent pays recoupment amount to DWP, remainder to claimant

**This is NOT a reduction in award** - it's a mechanism for government to recover benefits paid. Claimant doesn't receive those sums twice.

## LEGAL FRAMEWORKS - QUICK REFERENCE

### Reasonable Adjustments (s.20-21 EqA 2010)

**Three-limbed duty:**
1. **PCP** causing substantial disadvantage
2. **Physical feature** causing substantial disadvantage
3. **Auxiliary aid** needed

**Five-step analysis:**
1. Did PCP/feature/lack of aid exist?
2. Did it place disabled person at substantial disadvantage compared to non-disabled persons?
3. Did employer know, or should they reasonably have known, person was disabled and disadvantaged?
4. Was there a step employer could have taken to avoid disadvantage?
5. Was it reasonable for employer to have to take that step?

**"Substantial disadvantage"** = more than minor or trivial (FirstGroup v Paulley [2017])

**Knowledge:** Actual OR constructive (Environment Agency v Rowan [2008])

**Reasonableness factors:**
- Practicability
- Effectiveness in preventing disadvantage
- Cost
- Employer's financial/other resources
- Availability of financial/other assistance
- Extent of disruption
- Nature and size of employer

**Key principle (Archibald v Fife Council [2004]):**
Duty may require employer to treat disabled person MORE favourably - e.g., exempting from competitive process, providing additional support

**Cross-reference:** See Glossary for "PCP" definition

### Direct Discrimination (s.13 EqA 2010)

**Three-part test:**
1. Less favourable treatment
2. Because of protected characteristic
3. Than comparator whose circumstances are not materially different

**Comparator:**
- Can be real (named person) or hypothetical
- Circumstances must NOT be materially different except for protected characteristic
- In disability cases: comparator is non-disabled person (or person without that particular disability)

**Causation:**
Protected characteristic must be effective cause of treatment. Can be:
- Conscious or unconscious
- One of multiple reasons (not just "but for" test)
- Proved by evidence or inference

**No justification defense** for direct disability discrimination (unlike s.15)

**Burden of proof:** Igen v Wong two-stage test (see section 2 above)

### Discrimination Arising from Disability (s.15 EqA 2010)

**Test:**
1. **Unfavourable treatment** (not "less favourable" - no comparator needed)
2. **Because of something arising in consequence of disability**
3. **Knowledge:** Employer knew or should have known person had disability
4. **Justification:** Can employer show treatment was proportionate means of achieving legitimate aim?

**Two-stage causation:**
- Stage 1: Does the "something" arise in consequence of disability?
- Stage 2: Was unfavourable treatment because of that "something"?

**Example:**
Disability: Depression → Arising consequence: Absence from work → Unfavourable treatment: Dismissal for absence

**Knowledge requirement:**
Employer must have known (actual or constructive) that person had THE DISABILITY, not necessarily that the "something" arose from it.

**Justification defense:**
Unlike s.13, employer can defend by showing treatment was proportionate means of achieving legitimate aim.

**Proportionality test (Hardy & Hansons v Lax [2005]):**
- Was aim legitimate?
- Was treatment appropriate and reasonably necessary to achieve that aim?
- Could less discriminatory alternative have achieved the aim?

**Cross-reference:** See Constructive Knowledge in Glossary

### Indirect Discrimination (s.19 EqA 2010)

**Four-part test:**
1. **PCP applied** to claimant and others
2. **Group disadvantage:** PCP puts (or would put) persons sharing protected characteristic at particular disadvantage
3. **Individual disadvantage:** PCP puts claimant at that disadvantage
4. **Justification:** Employer cannot show PCP is proportionate means of achieving legitimate aim

**Key points:**
- Must show GROUP disadvantage, not just individual (need statistical/other evidence)
- Pool for comparison: who does PCP apply to?
- "Particular disadvantage" means substantial disadvantage

**Example:**
PCP: Full-time working requirement
Group disadvantage: Women more likely to have childcare responsibilities, less likely to work full-time
Individual: Female claimant can't work full-time due to childcare
Justification: Employer must show why full-time requirement is proportionate

### Harassment (s.26 EqA 2010)

**Test:**
1. **Unwanted conduct**
2. **Related to** protected characteristic (looser than "because of")
3. **Purpose or effect** of:
   - Violating claimant's dignity, OR
   - Creating intimidating, hostile, degrading, humiliating or offensive environment

**Effect assessment (s.26(4)):**
Must consider:
- Claimant's perception (subjective)
- Other circumstances of case
- Whether reasonable for conduct to have that effect (objective)

**Not harassment if:**
- Conduct is reasonable in circumstances (objective)

**Key difference from other discrimination:** No need to show comparator. Focus on impact of conduct.

**"Related to"** is broader than "because of" - includes association and perception cases.

### Victimisation (s.27 EqA 2010)

**Test:**
1. **Protected act** done (or employer believes person will do protected act)
2. **Detriment** suffered
3. **Because of** protected act

**Protected acts:**
- Bringing proceedings under EqA 2010
- Giving evidence/information in connection with EqA proceedings
- Doing any other thing for purposes of/in connection with EqA 2010
- Making allegation (express or implied) that someone breached EqA 2010

**Causation (Nagarajan v London Regional Transport [2000]):**
Protected act must have "significant influence" on treatment - not necessarily sole or main reason, but more than trivial

**Exception:**
No victimisation if protected act involved false allegation made in bad faith

**Example:**
Employee raises grievance alleging disability discrimination (protected act) → Subsequently denied promotion (detriment) → Need to show denial was because of grievance

### Constructive Dismissal

**Four-part test:**
1. **Employer breached contract** (usually implied term of trust and confidence)
2. **Breach was fundamental** (sufficiently serious)
3. **Employee resigned in response to breach** (not for other reason)
4. **Employee didn't affirm contract** (didn't wait too long/act inconsistently)

**Implied term of trust and confidence (Malik v BCCI [1997]):**
"Employer shall not, without reasonable and proper cause, conduct itself in a manner calculated or likely to destroy or seriously damage the relationship of trust and confidence"

**Last straw doctrine (Omilaju v Waltham Forest [2005]):**
- Final act can be "last straw" in series of acts
- Last straw must contribute to breach (not wholly innocuous)
- Cumulative effect assessed

**Common mistake:**
Resigning immediately is NOT required - but delay may suggest affirmation. Generally, should resign within reasonable time (weeks, not months) after breach.

**If successful:** Treated as dismissal for unfair dismissal purposes

## DISCLOSURE AND EVIDENCE PRINCIPLES

### Disclosure Obligations

**Standard:** Documents relevant to issues in case that might reasonably be expected to support either party's case.

**Tribunal's power:** Can order disclosure (Employment Tribunal Rules, Rule 31)

**Types of failure:**
1. Non-disclosure (withholding relevant documents)
2. Late disclosure (providing at last minute, prejudicing preparation)
3. Incomplete disclosure (redacted or partial)
4. Lack of candour (misleading description/presentation)

**Consequences:**
- Adverse inferences drawn
- Unless orders (comply or claim/defense struck out)
- Costs orders in serious cases

### Legal Privilege - What's Protected

**Legal advice privilege:**
Confidential communications between lawyer and client for purpose of giving/receiving legal advice

**Litigation privilege:**
Confidential communications for dominant purpose of actual or contemplated litigation

**Common mistakes (NOT privileged):**
- Operational HR decisions
- Factual investigations (unless for litigation)
- Policy discussions
- Communications with non-lawyers (e.g., HR)

**Privilege belongs to client** (employer if in-house lawyer) and can be waived

### Personal Injury & Tort Nature of Discrimination Remedy

**Important principle:** Discrimination awards are **tortious compensation**, not contractual damages.

**What this means:**
1. **No statutory cap** (unlike unfair dismissal)
2. **Aim:** Restore claimant to position they'd be in BUT FOR discrimination
3. **Includes:**
   - Injury to feelings (Vento)
   - Psychiatric injury (if caused by discrimination)
   - Financial loss (past and future)
   - Aggravated damages (in exceptional cases)
4. **Standard of proof:** Civil standard (balance of probabilities)
5. **Causation:** Discrimination must be effective cause of loss

**Injury to Feelings (Vento v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire [2003]):**

**Current bands (2024/25 - updated annually for inflation):**
- **Lower band:** £1,200 - £7,200 (less serious, isolated/one-off)
- **Middle band:** £7,200 - £21,600 (serious, not meriting top band)
- **Upper band:** £21,600 - £36,000 (most serious)
- **Exceptional:** Over £36,000 (very rare)

**Factors:**
- Nature and extent of discrimination
- Context and vulnerability of claimant
- Impact on claimant
- Aggravating factors (malice, failure to take complaints seriously)
- Mitigating factors (apology, steps to prevent recurrence)

**Psychiatric Injury:**
Separate from injury to feelings. Requires:
- Medical expert evidence (usually psychiatrist)
- Diagnosis (depression, anxiety, PTSD, etc.)
- Causation linking to discrimination
- Prognosis
- Awards based on Judicial College Guidelines (same as personal injury claims)

## PROCEDURAL STAGES - DETAILED GUIDANCE

### Employment Tribunal Process

**1. Early Conciliation (ACAS) - MANDATORY**

**What it is:**
Free, confidential conciliation service. ACAS-appointed conciliator helps parties try to settle dispute without tribunal.

**Process:**
```
Employee contacts ACAS online/phone
    ↓
ACAS notifies employer
    ↓
TIME LIMIT CLOCK STOPS (extended)
    ↓
Conciliation period (usually up to 6 weeks)
    ↓
    ├─ Settlement reached → COT3 agreement (legally binding)
    │                        Case ends
    │
    └─ No settlement → EC certificate issued
                        Can now file ET1
                        Extended time limit applies
```

**Time limit effect:**
- Minimum extension: 1 month
- Clock stops on day ACAS notified
- Restarts day after EC certificate issued
- Usually gives extra ~1 month total

**"Without prejudice":** Discussions in EC are confidential, can't be used in tribunal

**2. Claim Submission (ET1)**

**Deadline:** Within 3 months minus 1 day of:
- Act complained of (if one-off act), OR
- Last act in series (if continuing act), OR
- End of EC period (see time limit calculator above)

**Contents must include:**
- Your details and respondent's details
- Type of claim (tick boxes)
- Details of claim (narrative - can be detailed or use "further particulars to follow")
- What remedy you seek
- EC certificate number

**Submission:** Online via gov.uk portal (preferred) or by post

**After submission:**
- Tribunal sends copy to respondent
- Respondent has 28 days to submit ET3 (response)

**3. Response (ET3)**

**Respondent's deadline:** 28 days from receiving ET1

**Can request extension** (usually only granted once, for good reason)

**If no ET3 filed:** Respondent can't defend claim (default judgment possible)

**If ET3 filed:** Tribunal proceeds to case management

**4. Case Management**

**Preliminary Hearing (usually first step):**
- Usually held by phone/video
- Judge (sitting alone usually)
- Identifies issues, gives orders, sets hearing date
- Private hearing

**Standard case management orders:**
- Disclosure (each party lists/provides documents)
- Witness statements (written statements from each witness)
- Agreed bundle (all documents, paginated, indexed)
- List of issues (what tribunal will decide)
- Final hearing date set

**Unless orders:**
"Unless you comply by [date], your claim/response will be struck out"
- Serious consequences for non-compliance
- Can apply for extension if needed (before deadline if possible)

**5. Final Hearing**

**Format:**
- Public hearing (anyone can attend)
- Usually 1-5 days depending on complexity
- Judge + two lay members (panel of 3) OR Judge alone (if parties agree/discrimination-only claims)

**Process:**
```
Opening submissions (optional)
    ↓
Claimant's evidence:
  - Witness statement taken as evidence-in-chief (already read)
  - Cross-examination by respondent
  - Re-examination by claimant (clarification only)
  - Questions from tribunal
    ↓
Respondent's evidence:
  - Same process for each witness
    ↓
Closing submissions:
  - Claimant first (bringing claim)
  - Respondent responds
  - Claimant's right of reply (if needed)
    ↓
Tribunal deliberates (private)
    ↓
Judgment (oral or reserved)
```

**Reserved judgment:** Tribunal needs time to consider; written judgment sent later (usually within weeks)

**Oral judgment:** Decision announced at hearing; can request written reasons

**6. Judgment**

**Liability judgment** (separate from remedy if remedy not determined at same hearing)

**Tribunal decides:**
- Are claims upheld or dismissed?
- Findings of fact
- Application of law to facts
- Reasons for decision

**Written reasons:** Always provided for final hearings (automatically or on request)

**What to do:**
- Read judgment carefully
- If claims upheld: proceed to remedy (if not already determined)
- If claims dismissed: consider appeal (42 days) or reconsideration (14 days)
- If partially successful: may appeal dismissed claims while proceeding to remedy on successful claims

**7. Remedy Hearing (if not determined with liability)**

**Preparation:**

**Schedule of Loss (claimant prepares):**
```
1. Injury to Feelings: £[amount] (specify Vento band)
2. Personal Injury: £[amount] (attach medical evidence)
3. Past Financial Loss:
   - Net monthly pay: £X
   - Period of loss: [date] to [date] = Y months
   - Less mitigation: £Z
   - Total past loss: £[calculation]
4. Future Loss: £[amount] (if applicable, with multiplier)
5. Pension Loss: £[amount]
6. Interest: £[amount]
7. ACAS Uplift: [X%] if applicable
---
TOTAL: £[sum]
```

**Counter-schedule (respondent responds):**
Challenges amounts, proposes reductions (Polkey, contribution)

**Evidence at remedy hearing:**
- Medical evidence (if personal injury claimed)
- Evidence of mitigation efforts (job search)
- Evidence of actual loss
- Witness evidence if needed

**Judgment on remedy:** Specifies amounts awarded under each head

**Cross-reference:** See Remedy Reductions section for Polkey, contribution, recoupment

## EMPLOYMENT APPEAL TRIBUNAL (EAT)

**Jurisdiction:** Appeals on **points of law ONLY** - not findings of fact

**What qualifies as error of law:**
- Tribunal applied wrong legal test
- Misunderstood or misapplied legal principle
- Failed to consider relevant matter or considered irrelevant matter
- Decision perverse (no reasonable tribunal could have reached it)
- Procedural unfairness
- Inadequate reasons

**What does NOT qualify:**
- Disagreeing with tribunal's factual findings
- Tribunal believed other side's witnesses
- Different view of evidence

**Process:**

**1. Notice of Appeal**
- **Deadline:** 42 days from date written judgment sent (strict)
- **Form:** EAT Form 1 (available on gov.uk)
- **Contents:**
  - Identify tribunal's judgment being appealed
  - State grounds of appeal (specific errors of law)
  - Why those errors make judgment wrong
- **Fee:** £400 (2024/25) (remission available if low income)

**2. Rule 3(10) Sift**

EAT judge reviews Notice of Appeal on paper:

**Three possible outcomes:**
1. **Proceed to full hearing** - arguable error of law identified
2. **Rule 3(10) hearing** - borderline; appellant gets oral hearing to argue appeal should proceed
3. **Dismissed** - no reasonable grounds; appeal stops here (unless appeal to Court of Appeal)

**3. Rule 3(10) Hearing (if applicable)**

- Short hearing (~30 mins)
- Appellant argues appeal has merit
- Judge decides: proceed to full hearing OR dismiss

**4. Full Hearing (if appeal allowed to proceed)**

- Oral argument on law
- Both parties' barristers/representatives present legal arguments
- No witness evidence (EAT doesn't re-hear facts)

**EAT can:**
- **Dismiss appeal** (tribunal's judgment stands)
- **Allow appeal and substitute decision** (EAT decides outcome)
- **Remit to tribunal** (send back to tribunal - same judge or different judge)

**5. Further Appeal to Court of Appeal**

- Needs permission (from EAT or Court of Appeal)
- Only on important point of law of general public importance

**Practical advice:**
- EAT appeals are highly technical
- Strongly recommended to have barrister representation
- Free Representation Unit (FRU) may assist
- Success rate is low (many appeals dismissed at sift stage)

**Cross-reference:** See User Journey Pathway 5

## COMMON MISTAKES - AVOID THESE

### Procedural Mistakes

**1. Missing time limits**
- ❌ Thinking 3 months means "before the end of the 3rd month"
- ✅ It's 3 months MINUS ONE DAY from the discriminatory act
- ❌ Forgetting to contact ACAS before filing
- ✅ ACAS Early Conciliation is MANDATORY first step

**2. Inadequate particulars in ET1**
- ❌ Writing "I was discriminated against" without details
- ✅ Specify: dates, acts, people involved, which type of discrimination (s.13/15/19/20/26/27)

**3. Not preserving evidence**
- ❌ Deleting emails, not keeping notes
- ✅ Keep contemporaneous records, emails, messages; back up everything

**4. Breaching without prejudice**
- ❌ Mentioning settlement discussions from ACAS EC at tribunal
- ✅ Those discussions are confidential and inadmissible

**5. Not following tribunal orders**
- ❌ Missing disclosure deadline or ignoring unless order
- ✅ Comply with orders or apply for extension BEFORE deadline

### Substantive Mistakes

**6. Wrong comparator in s.13 claims**
- ❌ Comparing yourself with someone in materially different circumstances
- ✅ Comparator's circumstances must be same except for disability

**7. Confusing s.13 and s.15**
- ❌ Using s.13 when treatment was because of absence (consequence of disability), not disability itself
- ✅ s.13 = because of disability itself; s.15 = because of something arising from disability

**8. Not requesting adjustments before claiming failure**
- ❌ Assuming employer should have known what adjustments needed without asking
- ✅ While employer should make enquiries, clear communication helps your case

**9. Forgetting knowledge element in s.15 and s.20-21**
- ❌ Claiming discrimination when employer had no idea about disability
- ✅ Must show employer knew or should have known (constructive knowledge)

**10. Claiming injury to feelings without impact evidence**
- ❌ Saying "I felt upset" with no details
- ✅ Evidence of impact: GP visits, counseling, effect on daily life, witness testimony

### Evidence Mistakes

**11. Purely subjective statements**
- ❌ "I felt they discriminated against me"
- ✅ Facts: "On [date], [person] said [words]. This made me feel [X] because [Y]. The impact was [Z]."

**12. Not addressing respondent's explanation**
- ❌ Ignoring employer's stated reason for treatment
- ✅ Explain why stated reason is not true reason (pretext) or is discriminatory

**13. Weak witness statements**
- ❌ Vague, undated, opinion-based
- ✅ Chronological, specific dates/times, what you saw/heard, how it affected you

### Remedy Mistakes

**14. Not mitigating loss**
- ❌ Not looking for new work after dismissal
- ✅ Duty to mitigate - make reasonable efforts to find work; keep records of job applications

**15. Forgetting to deduct mitigation**
- ❌ Claiming full loss while earning in new job
- ✅ Deduct earnings from new employment from past loss calculation

**16. No medical evidence for personal injury**
- ❌ Claiming £20k psychiatric injury without expert report
- ✅ Need medical expert (usually psychiatrist) report for personal injury claims

**17. Unrealistic Vento bands**
- ❌ Claiming top band for minor, one-off incident
- ✅ Understand bands: lower (one-off/minor), middle (serious), upper (most serious)

### Appeal Mistakes

**18. Appealing findings of fact**
- ❌ "The tribunal should have believed my evidence, not theirs"
- ✅ EAT only hears errors of LAW, not challenges to fact-finding

**19. Missing 42-day appeal deadline**
- ❌ Thinking about it for months before appealing
- ✅ 42 days is strict; start preparing immediately if considering appeal

**20. Not getting legal advice on appeal**
- ❌ Appealing without understanding what's an error of law
- ✅ EAT appeals are technical; strongly advisable to get barrister advice

### Which Type of Discrimination?

```
I was treated badly at work due to my disability...

QUESTION 1: Was treatment BECAUSE OF the disability itself, or because of
            something ARISING FROM the disability?

├─ BECAUSE OF DISABILITY ITSELF
│  Example: "We don't hire autistic people"
│  └─ DIRECT DISCRIMINATION (s.13)
│     - No justification defense
│     - Need comparator (non-disabled person in same circumstances)
│     - High bar to prove
│
└─ BECAUSE OF SOMETHING ARISING FROM DISABILITY
   Example: "We dismissed you for absence" (absence caused by disability)
   └─ DISCRIMINATION ARISING FROM DISABILITY (s.15)
      - Justification defense available (employer can argue proportionate)
      - No comparator needed
      - Employer must have known about disability

QUESTION 2: Was there a rule/practice that disadvantaged me?

└─ YES → Was it:
   │
   ├─ Neutral rule that disadvantages disabled people generally?
   │  Example: "Everyone must work full-time in office"
   │  └─ INDIRECT DISCRIMINATION (s.19)
   │     - Need to show group disadvantage (disabled people generally)
   │     - Justification defense available
   │
   └─ Rule/practice that put me at substantial disadvantage, and employer
      didn't make reasonable adjustment?
      Example: "No scheduled breaks" + employer refused to adjust
      └─ FAILURE TO MAKE REASONABLE ADJUSTMENTS (s.20-21)
         - Employer must have known about disability and disadvantage
         - NO justification defense (if adjustment reasonable, must make it)
         - Often strongest claim

QUESTION 3: Was there unwanted conduct creating hostile environment?

└─ YES → HARASSMENT (s.26)
   - Related to disability
   - Violated dignity or created hostile/humiliating environment
   - Subjective + objective test

QUESTION 4: Did bad treatment happen BECAUSE I complained about discrimination?

└─ YES → VICTIMISATION (s.27)
   - Protected act: raising grievance, bringing claim, giving evidence
   - Detriment because of protected act
   - Nagarajan causation test
```
