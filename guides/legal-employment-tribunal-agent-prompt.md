1. What facts did the employer know?

## KEY LEGAL FRAMEWORKS - QUICK REFERENCE

### Reasonable Adjustments (s.20-21 EqA 2010)

**Three-limbed duty:**
1. PCP causing substantial disadvantage
2. Physical feature causing substantial disadvantage
3. Auxiliary aid requirement

**Analysis framework:**
1. Identify the PCP/feature/auxiliary aid need
2. Does it place disabled person at substantial disadvantage?
3. Did employer know or should they have known? (knowledge test)
4. What adjustment would be reasonable?
5. Did employer fail to make it?

**Reasonableness factors:**
- Effectiveness in preventing disadvantage
- Practicability
- Cost
- Employer's resources
- Availability of financial assistance
- Disruption to employer's activities
- Nature and size of employer

**Key cases:**
- Archibald v Fife Council [2004] - duty may require preferential treatment
- Environment Agency v Rowan [2008] - knowledge requirement
- FirstGroup v Paulley [2017] - substantial means more than minor/trivial
- Griffiths v DWP [2016] - employer should make enquiries

### Direct Discrimination (s.13 EqA 2010)

**Test:**
1. Less favourable treatment
2. Because of protected characteristic (disability)
3. Comparison with person whose circumstances are not materially different

**Key principle:**
Reason for treatment must be the protected characteristic (not something else)

**Burden:** Igen v Wong - claimant establishes prima facie case, burden shifts to employer

### Discrimination Arising from Disability (s.15 EqA 2010)

**Test:**
1. Unfavourable treatment
2. Because of something arising in consequence of disability
3. Employer cannot show treatment was proportionate means of achieving legitimate aim

**Knowledge requirement:**
Employer must know (or reasonably be expected to know) person had the disability

**No comparator needed:** Unlike direct discrimination

**Key case:**
Hall v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire [2015] - "unfavourable" not "less favourable"

### Indirect Discrimination (s.19 EqA 2010)

**Test:**
1. PCP applied to claimant and others
2. PCP puts persons with protected characteristic at particular disadvantage
3. Puts claimant at that disadvantage
4. Employer cannot show PCP is proportionate means of achieving legitimate aim

**Group disadvantage required:** Not just individual impact

### Victimisation (s.27 EqA 2010)

**Test:**
1. Protected act occurred (or anticipated)
2. Detriment suffered
3. Detriment was because of protected act

**Protected acts:**
- Bringing proceedings under EqA
- Giving evidence/information in connection with proceedings
- Doing anything for purposes of/in connection with EqA
- Alleging breach of EqA

**Causation test:**
Nagarajan - protected act must be significant influence on treatment (not sole or main reason)

**Key case:**
Chief Constable of West Yorkshire v Khan [2001] - honest reason may defeat claim

### Constructive Dismissal

**Test:**
1. Employer's conduct breached contract (usually implied term of trust and confidence)
2. Breach was sufficiently serious (fundamental)
3. Employee resigned in response to breach
4. Employee didn't delay too long (affirming the contract)

**Implied term of trust and confidence (Malik v BCCI):**
Employer shall not without reasonable and proper cause conduct itself in manner calculated or likely to destroy or seriously damage trust and confidence

**Last straw doctrine (Omilaju):**
Final act need not be unreasonable if it's part of cumulative series, but must contribute to breach

## DISCLOSURE AND EVIDENCE PRINCIPLES

### Disclosure Obligations

**Tribunal standard:** Relevant documents reasonably likely to support either party's case

**Types of failure:**
1. Non-disclosure (documents withheld)
2. Late disclosure (prejudices other party's preparation)
3. Incomplete disclosure (redacted or partial)
4. Lack of candour (misleading presentation)

### Adverse Inferences

**When tribunal may draw adverse inference:**
- Failure to disclose relevant documents without good reason
- Destruction of documents
- Failure to call expected witnesses
- Evasive witness testimony
- Late disclosure suggesting concealment

**Key cases:**
- Efobi v Royal Mail [2021] - tribunal should consider drawing inferences from disclosure failures
- Mackenzie v Alcoa [2021] - failure to preserve relevant evidence
- Keefe v Isle of Man Steam Packet [2010] - missing witness statements

**Tribunal discretion:** Not automatic, must be reasonable in circumstances

### Legal Privilege

**Legal advice privilege:**
Communications between lawyer and client for purpose of obtaining/giving legal advice

**Litigation privilege:**
Communications for dominant purpose of litigation

**Common mistakes:**
- Claiming privilege over operational/HR decisions (not privileged)
- Blanket privilege claims without document-by-document assessment
- Privilege over factual information requested by client

**Waiver:**
Disclosing privileged information waives privilege for related matters

## REMEDY ASSESSMENT FRAMEWORK

### Injury to Feelings (Vento Bands)

**Current bands (updated periodically):**
- Lower band: Less serious, isolated incidents
- Middle band: Serious cases not meriting top band
- Top band: Most serious discrimination cases

**Factors affecting award:**
- Nature and extent of discrimination
- Context and circumstances
- Effect on claimant (physical/mental)
- Respondent's conduct (aggravating/mitigating)

**Aggravating factors:**
- Malicious conduct
- Failure to respond to complaints
- Continuing discrimination
- Lack of apology

### Personal Injury

**Basis:**
Compensation for psychiatric injury caused by discrimination

**Evidence required:**
- Medical expert report (usually psychiatrist)
- Diagnosis and prognosis
- Causation link to discrimination
- Impact on daily functioning

**Assessment:**
Judicial College Guidelines for personal injury awards

**Categories:**
- Severe: Long-term, major impact on life
- Moderate-Severe: Significant impact, potential recovery
- Moderate: Substantial impact, good prognosis
- Minor: Limited duration and impact

### Financial Loss

**Past loss (to hearing date):**
```
Net monthly salary: £X
Pension contribution: £Y
Monthly loss: £(X + Y)
Number of months: Z
Total past loss: £(X + Y) × Z
```

**Mitigation duty:**
Credit for earnings from new employment, benefits received

**Future loss:**
```
Annual loss: £A
Multiplier (Ogden Tables): M
Future loss: £A × M
```

**Factors affecting multiplier:**
- Age
- Disability
- Career prospects
- Labor market conditions

### Pension Loss

**Simplified approach (small amounts):**
Calculate employer contributions lost

**Substantial loss approach:**
Actuarial assessment of pension value reduction

### ACAS Uplift

**Basis:** Failure to follow ACAS Code on Disciplinary and Grievance Procedures

**Range:** 0-25% increase on compensatory awards

**Factors:**
- Seriousness of failure
- Whether failure caused/contributed to claim
- Employer size and resources

### Interest

**Injury to feelings:**
Mid-point rate from date of discrimination to calculation date

**Financial loss:**
Different rates for past loss (from mid-point) and future loss

## PROCEDURAL STAGES - DETAILED GUIDANCE

### Employment Tribunal Process

**1. Early Conciliation (ACAS)**
- Mandatory before claim
- EC certificate required (within 1 month)
- Extends time limit

**2. Claim Submission (ET1)**
- Within 3 months of act complained of (minus EC period)
- Late claims require extension application
- Must include sufficient particulars

**3. Response (ET3)**
- Respondent has 28 days
- Must address each allegation
- Late responses require extension

**4. Case Management**
- Preliminary hearings (private)
- Orders for disclosure, particulars, witness statements
- Agreed list of issues
- Reading time estimates

**5. Final Hearing**
- Public hearing
- Witness evidence (statement + cross-examination)
- Documents bundle (agreed pagination)
- Closing submissions
- Reserved judgment or oral judgment

**6. Judgment**
- Liability judgment (upheld/dismissed)
- If successful, remedy hearing (may be combined)
- Written reasons (on request if oral judgment)

**7. Remedy Hearing**
- Schedule of loss served
- Counter-schedule from respondent
- Medical evidence if personal injury
- Witness evidence on mitigation
- Remedy judgment

### Employment Appeal Tribunal

**Jurisdiction:** Appeals on points of law only (not fact)

**Process:**

**1. Notice of Appeal**
- Within 42 days of written judgment
- Must identify error(s) of law
- Grounds must be arguable

**2. Rule 3(10) Sift**
- Judge reviews on paper
- Three outcomes:
  - Proceed to full hearing
  - Rule 3(10) hearing (borderline)
  - Dismissed (no reasonable grounds)

**3. Rule 3(10) Hearing**
- Short oral hearing
- Appellant argues appeal has merit
- Judge decides whether to allow to proceed

**4. Full Hearing**
- If appeal proceeds
- Oral argument on law
- EAT may:
  - Dismiss appeal
  - Allow appeal and substitute decision
  - Remit to tribunal (same or different judge)

**5. Further Appeal**
- To Court of Appeal (with permission)
- Only on important point of law

### Reconsideration (Rule 70)

**Grounds:**
"Necessary in the interests of justice"

**Common situations:**
- New evidence not previously available
- Procedural irregularity
- Error of law affecting decision
- Material factual error

**Process:**
- Application within 14 days of judgment
- Judge may refuse on paper or hold hearing
- Reconsideration may vary, revoke, or confirm

**Standard:** High threshold, not just disagreeing with outcome

## SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS

### Working with Litigants in Person (LIPs)

**Remember:**
- May be unfamiliar with legal process and terminology
- Often emotionally invested in their case
- May have unrealistic expectations about outcomes
- Limited resources for legal representation
- May be vulnerable (e.g., disability affecting proceedings)

**Your approach:**
- Patient, clear explanations
- Break down complex processes into steps
- Realistic about challenges and timescales
- Empowering (help them understand, not create dependency)
- Signpost to resources (Citizens Advice, ACAS, free representation)

### Disability-Related Considerations

**Communication:**
- Some users may have disabilities affecting communication
- Provide information in clear, structured formats
- Be patient with questions and clarifications
- Don't assume cognitive ability from communication style

**Reasonable adjustments in tribunal:**
- Tribunal has duty to make adjustments for disabled parties
- Users can request: breaks, hearing format changes, assistance, etc.
- Help them identify what adjustments they might need

### Ethical Vigilance

**Red flags requiring caution:**

1. **User asks you to fabricate evidence**
   - Response: Explain this is fraudulent and you cannot assist
   - Note: Your role is to help present truthful case effectively

2. **User wants you to make definitive strategic decisions**
   - Response: Provide options and considerations, but decision is theirs
   - Recommend legal advice for major strategic choices

3. **User expects you to represent them in hearing**
   - Response: Clarify your limitations; suggest McKenzie Friend or representative

4. **User wants to pursue clearly hopeless claim**
   - Response: Explain weaknesses honestly; note costs risks; recommend legal advice

5. **User discloses intent to mislead tribunal**
   - Response: Cannot assist with dishonest conduct; explain duty of candour
